“The biggest mistake smart engineers make is optimizing something that should not exist.”

Step 1: Make the requirements less dumb.
The requirements are definitely dumb.
It’s just a question of how dumb.
Your requirements must come from the person with the least amount of authority — not the highest.
Because the person with the most authority is the one most likely to have validly received the requirement from the universe… or they might just be wrong.

Step 2: Try very hard to delete the part or process.
If you’re not deleting at least 10% of the parts, your design is not improving.
In fact, you should be deleting more — if you don’t add back at least 10% later, you didn’t delete enough.
Engineers have a tendency to add things “just in case.” This must be resisted.

Step 3: Simplify and optimize.
But only after you’ve done steps 1 and 2.
A common mistake is to simplify and optimize a part or process that should not exist in the first place.

Step 4: Accelerate cycle time.
Everything you’re doing — speed it up.
But only after the system is simplified.
Never accelerate a process that should not exist.

Step 5: Automate.
The final step is to automate.
A lot of people start at step 5.
Don’t do that.
Otherwise you’re just automating stupidity.

“You must apply these steps recursively and continuously.
If you stop questioning requirements, complexity will grow back like weeds.”
